Non-sovereign monarchies Main article: Non-sovereign monarchy (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-sovereign_monarchy) A non-sovereign monarchy is one where the monarch is subject to a temporal authority higher than their own. Some are dependent on other powers (see vassals (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal), suzerainty (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty), puppet state (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state), hegemony (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony)). In the British colonial era, indirect rule (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_rule) under a paramount power (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_ruler) existed, such as the princely states (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) under the British Raj (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). In Botswana (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana), South Africa (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa), Ghana (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana) and Uganda (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda), the ancient kingdoms and chiefdoms (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom) that were met by the colonialists when they first arrived on the continent are now constitutionally protected as regional or sectional entities. Furthermore, in Nigeria (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria), though the hundreds of sub-regional polities (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_traditional_states) that exist there are not provided for in the current constitution, they are nevertheless legally recognised aspects of the structure of governance that operates in the nation. For example, the Yoruba (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) city-state of Akure (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akure_Kingdom) in south-western Nigeria is something of an elective monarchy: its reigning Oba (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_(ruler)) Deji (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akure_Kingdom) has to be chosen by an electoral college of nobles (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Chieftaincy) from amongst a finite collection of royal princes of the realm upon the death or removal of an incumbent. In addition to these five countries, non-sovereign monarchies of varied sizes and complexities exist all over the rest of the continent of Africa (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa).
